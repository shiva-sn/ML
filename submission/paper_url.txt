    <!-- ── FORMAL ABSTRACT ── -->
    <section id="abstract" class="abstract-box">
      <div class="abstract-title">Abstract</div>
      <p class="abstract-body">
        This paper investigates what performance archetypes exist across a multi-client content portfolio and how those patterns can support editorial review triage. Using an anonymized inventory of <strong>418,047 unique content assets</strong> across <strong>84 client domains</strong> from the FlyRank internship warehouse, we construct a content-level modeling matrix over a rolling 90-day performance snapshot. Eight observed numeric signals—measuring search volume demand, content word count, creation age, elapsed update time, 90-day search impressions, click-through rate, impression-weighted average position, and user engagement rate—are preprocessed via zero-position correction (mapping 0 to NaN), median imputation, selective log1p transformation, and RobustScaling. An unsupervised K-Means clustering model (K=3) achieves an internal silhouette separation of <strong>0.8414</strong>, compared with <strong>0.4172</strong> for a simpler 3-feature baseline. Three interpretable structural patterns emerge: Low-Visibility Established Content (~89.7%), Stale Underperforming Content (~10.2%), and High-CTR Efficient Niche Content (~0.1%). Assignments feed a human-in-the-loop review queue and do not represent automated publishing decisions or causal ranking predictions.
      </p>
    </section>

    <section id="overview" class="paper-section">
      <div class="sec-num">Section 1</div>
      <h2 class="sec-heading">Research Overview &amp; Boundary of Claims</h2>
      <p>
        This project applies unsupervised K-Means clustering to structured content-level observations from the FlyRank internship warehouse. Eight observed features covering search demand, content size, freshness, search visibility, click-through rate, search position, and engagement are used to identify data-driven content archetypes.
      </p>
      <p>
        Three clusters emerge in the analyzed snapshot. Cluster labels are interpretations of observed patterns rather than predefined ground-truth classes. The analysis does not attempt to predict future Google ranking shifts, nor does it generate automatic content edits.
      </p>

      <div class="remark-box danger">
        <div class="remark-header">Methodological Guardrail — No Causal Claims</div>
        <div class="remark-body">
          This analysis reports observed and measured associations within a single 90-day snapshot. It does not establish that content staleness causes ranking declines, nor that modifying a page will cause higher search traffic, CTR, or revenue. Cluster assignments provide descriptive decision support for human editorial review, not causal guarantees.
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <!-- ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ 02 PROBLEM FRAMING ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ -->
    <section id="framing" class="paper-section">
      <div class="sec-num">Section 2</div>
      <h2 class="sec-heading">Problem Framing &amp; Analytical Task</h2>
      <p>
        <strong>Research Question:</strong> What performance archetypes exist across a large multi-client content portfolio, and can those patterns be described as a small number of actionable content archetypes?
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Decision Supported:</strong> Which types of content should a content/SEO team review first, and what kind of review may be appropriate? When managing large inventories comprising hundreds of thousands of published articles, content teams cannot audit every page manually. Arbitrary heuristics (e.g., sorting solely by pageviews or word count) overlook complex interactions between search impressions, keyword position, and freshness.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Task Type:</strong> Descriptive, unsupervised segmentation. No pre-existing target labels exist; the clustering algorithm discovers latent geometric groupings from observed signal distributions.
      </p>
    </section>

      <div class="sec-num">Section 5</div>
      <h2 class="sec-heading">Data Quality, Preprocessing &amp; Leakage Controls</h2>
      <p>
        To prevent distortion from extreme values and protect against data contamination, the data pipeline follows a deterministic eight-stage transformation sequence.
      </p>

      <div class="pipeline-sequence">
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">1</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Warehouse Ingestion</h5>
            <p>Authenticated Parquet reads via DuckDB virtual views over Hugging Face dataset.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">2</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Content Grain Aggregation</h5>
            <p>Group daily logs and query breadth into a single row per content item (418,047 assets).</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">3</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Zero-Position Correction</h5>
            <p><code>avg_position_90d == 0</code> converted to <code>NaN</code>. A zero indicates "no position recorded," not top rank.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">4</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Median Imputation</h5>
            <p><code>SimpleImputer(strategy="median")</code> fills numeric missing values without right-skewed mean bias.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">5</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Selective log1p Transformation</h5>
            <p>Applied strictly to skewed volume/count variables: <code>search_volume</code>, <code>word_count</code>, <code>impressions_90d</code>.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">6</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>RobustScaler Normalization</h5>
            <p>RobustScaler is used because SEO volume and performance variables can be highly skewed and contain extreme observations. Median/IQR-based scaling reduces sensitivity to extreme values.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">7</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>K-Means Clustering</h5>
            <p>KMeans(n_clusters=3, random_state=42, n_init=10) fits centroids in 8-dimensional space.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="pipeline-row">
          <div class="pipeline-step-idx">8</div>
          <div class="pipeline-text">
            <h5>Human Editorial Triage</h5>
            <p>Outputs feed prioritized human review queues; no automated content publishing occurs.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <section id="model" class="paper-section">
      <div class="sec-num">Section 6</div>
      <h2 class="sec-heading">Model Selection &amp; Baseline Comparison</h2>
      <p>
        Cluster count K was selected using the project's documented composite selection procedure over K=3 through K=8:
      </p>

      <div class="remark-box neutral">
        <div class="remark-header">Composite Selection Procedure</div>
        <div class="remark-body">
          <p style="font-family: var(--font-mono); font-size: 0.84rem; margin-bottom: 0.4rem;">
            Composite Score = Silhouette<sub>norm</sub> − Dominance Penalty − Tiny-Cluster Penalty
          </p>
          <div style="font-size: 0.8rem; color: var(--ink-secondary); line-height: 1.5;">
            Where:
            <br />• <strong>Dominance Penalty:</strong> max(0, largest_cluster_pct/100 − 0.60) — penalizes partitions where a single cluster swallows &gt;60% of data.
            <br />• <strong>Tiny-Cluster Penalty:</strong> max(0, 0.02 − min_cluster_pct) × 5 — penalizes vanishing clusters (&lt;2% content) to avoid trivial outliers.
            <br />• <strong>Outcome:</strong> K=3 was selected because it produced strong separation (silhouette = 0.8414) while remaining interpretable as three distinct archetypes.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <section id="limitations" class="paper-section">
      <div class="sec-num">Section 11</div>
      <h2 class="sec-heading">Limitations &amp; Interpretation Boundaries</h2>
      <p>
        Scientific integrity requires documenting analytical boundaries explicitly:
      </p>

      <ul class="limits-list">
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">1</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Snapshot Limitation (Single 90-Day Window)</strong>
            <p>The analysis reflects a single 90-day observation window. Temporal persistence across multi-quarter lifecycles or seasonality has not been evaluated.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">2</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Observational Associations, Not Causal Drivers</strong>
            <p>Clusters describe observed associations. The analysis does not establish that staleness causes rank degradation, nor that editing pages guarantees traffic recovery.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">3</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Multi-Client Heterogeneity</strong>
            <p>The portfolio aggregates 84 clients with diverse site architectures and content strategies, which may introduce domain-specific baseline variations.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">4</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Rare Cluster Support (Cluster 2 ~0.1%)</strong>
            <p>Cluster 2 has very small support (~0.1%). Centroid distance diagnostics indicate boundary sensitivity, requiring case-level editorial verification.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">5</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Position Imputation Effects</strong>
            <p>Converting unranked positions (0) to NaN followed by median imputation affects cluster margins for zero-impression assets.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li class="limits-item">
          <div class="limits-idx">6</div>
          <div class="limits-content">
            <strong>Metric-Based, Not Semantic Text Analysis</strong>
            <p>Clustering is driven strictly by numeric performance metrics and does not inspect article prose, topical depth, or semantic writing quality.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
      </ul>
    </section>
